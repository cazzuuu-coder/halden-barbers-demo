About the room

The house is a study, written as if it were open.

Maison Café was designed as a portfolio piece: a complete little website for a café that does not exist, so that a real business can see the kind of work WEBAUR makes.

We drew this room for a portfolio, not for a street. Still, it should feel like a place you already know: the window that holds the afternoon, the sound of a cup set down, the loaf that is cut to order.

The name is French because so many neighbourhood rooms borrow a little of that language — a maison as a house, not as a claim to Paris. The writing is in English so the concept can be read clearly. The address, when you reach it, is invented on purpose.

What matters is the atmosphere. A café website often becomes a gallery of stock breakfasts and a list of opening hours. We wanted the page to behave more like a short essay with photographs: a few large stills, a careful typeface, and enough quiet that the food can be looked at properly.

Stay longer than you meant to. That is the only service the room is built to advertise.

The counter

The invented kitchen is modest on purpose. A short bakery list in the morning; soup and a green plate at midday; something sweet if the fruit is good. A longer menu would have made the concept look busier than the room we wanted to show.

The photographs were chosen for a single temperature — warm wood, pale cups, dark pastry, plants against glass — and cropped so that no other café’s name remains in the frame. They are freely licensed pictures, credited on this project, not a commissioned shoot.

Coffee, without a manifesto

There is no invented roaster here, and no list of medals. A concept that pretends to have won things is a poor way to ask for a client’s trust. The coffee is described the way a careful room would describe it: a filter when you have the morning, a latte when you want the cup to last, an iced glass for the long afternoon.

If WEBAUR were making this site for a real café, this chapter would be rewritten with the owner’s own words — the actual beans, the actual baker, the actual reason the door opens at eight. The structure would stay: a story you can sit with, not a block of marketing copy above a form.
